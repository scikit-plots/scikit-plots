# A Reusable Inquiry Cycle and Question Toolkit

Use this framework to investigate a question in any field: science, policy, history, business, architecture, design, or everyday problem-solving. It is iterative—not a one-way checklist. New evidence can send the inquiry back to an earlier stage.

## The inquiry cycle

**Observation and Problem ⇄ Hypothesis (Assumption) ⇄ Thesis (Proposition) ⇄ Antithesis (Critique and Counter-test) ⇄ Synthesis (Revised Explanation or Knowledge)**

| Stage | Purpose | Questions to ask | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Observation and problem** | Describe what is happening and define what needs explaining or changing. Separate observations from interpretations. | What have we observed? Where and when does it happen? Who is affected? What is the problem, and how do we know it is a problem? What is outside the scope? | A specific, evidence-grounded problem statement |
| **2. Hypothesis or assumption** | Offer a tentative explanation, prediction, or belief that could be checked. Make hidden assumptions visible. | What might explain the observation? What are we assuming? What would we expect to observe if this explanation were correct? What other explanations are plausible? | A testable hypothesis and a list of assumptions |
| **3. Thesis or proposition** | State the main claim clearly, including its conditions and implications. A thesis may be an argument or proposed solution, not necessarily a scientific hypothesis. | What exactly are we claiming? For whom, where, and under what conditions should it hold? What evidence supports it? What follows if it is true? | A clear claim, its scope, and supporting reasons |
| **4. Antithesis: critique and counter-test** | Look for ways the claim could be wrong, incomplete, or less useful than an alternative. Seek disconfirming evidence rather than only confirmation. | What is the strongest counterargument? Which observation would challenge the claim? What alternative explanation fits the evidence? Who or what does the claim fail to account for? | Counterevidence, limitations, alternatives, and tests |
| **5. Synthesis: revised explanation or knowledge** | Integrate the strongest evidence and criticism. Keep what survives, revise what does not, and identify what remains unresolved. | What survives the counter-test? What should be changed, narrowed, or rejected? Which explanation best fits the evidence? What remains uncertain, and what should be investigated next? | A revised claim, model, decision, or open question |

### Repeat the cycle

A synthesis is not necessarily a final answer. Treat it as a better-supported starting point for another round:

**Revised knowledge → new observations → sharper problem → updated hypothesis or proposition → further critique and testing**

Keep a record of what changed between rounds and why. This makes it easier to distinguish genuine learning from a change in opinion without new evidence.

## Question-word toolkit

The entries below include single-word interrogatives (what, when, where,
why, who, whom, whose, which, how) and common multiword question forms (how
many, what kind, under what conditions, and so on).

| Form | Useful for discovering | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **What** | Events, objects, meanings, requirements, or evidence | What changed before the problem appeared? |
| **What kind / what type** | Categories or characteristics | What kind of evidence would support this explanation? |
| **What time** | A specific time of day | What time does the effect usually begin? |
| **When** | Timing, sequence, or change over time | When did the pattern first appear? |
| **Where** | Location, scope, or distribution | Where does the pattern occur—and where does it not? |
| **Why** | Reasons, causes, or purposes | Why might this explanation account for the observation? |
| **Who** | People, groups, or agents | Who is affected, and who has relevant knowledge? |
| **Whom** | The person or group affected; formal in many contexts | Whom does this decision affect? |
| **Whose** | Ownership, responsibility, or perspective | Whose interests are represented in the evidence? |
| **Which** | Selecting among alternatives | Which competing explanation fits the facts best? |
| **How** | Process, method, or mechanism | How could the proposed cause produce the observed effect? |
| **How many** | Countable quantities | How many cases show the same pattern? |
| **How much** | Amount, degree, or cost | How much evidence supports the claim? |
| **How often** | Frequency or recurrence | How often does the result occur? |
| **How old** | Age or elapsed time | How old is the data, object, or system being studied? |
| **How long** | Duration or length | How long does the effect last? |
| **How far** | Distance or extent | How far does the effect extend? |
| **How likely** | Probability or confidence | How likely is this outcome under the alternative explanation? |
| **To what extent** | Degree or magnitude | To what extent does the result depend on context? |
| **Under what conditions** | Boundaries, dependencies, and exceptions | Under what conditions does the claim hold? |
| **Compared with what** | Baselines and alternatives | Compared with what baseline is this result meaningful? |
| **What if** | Scenarios and consequences | What if the key assumption is false? |
| **How might we** | Open-ended design or problem-solving | How might we address the problem without creating a new one? |

| Category | Count | Questions |
|---|---:|---|
| A questions | 1 | architecture |
| D questions | 1 | design |
| H questions | 5 | how, how many, how much, how often, how old |
| W questions | 10 | where, when, why, which, who, whose, whom, what, what time, what kind |

### Yes/no questions

Questions beginning with **is, are, was, were, do, does, did, can, could, will, would, should, have,** or **has** can check a specific condition:

- Is the observation repeatable?
- Does the proposed cause precede the outcome?
- Could another factor explain the result?
- Would the claim still hold in a different setting?

Use yes/no questions to check a defined point; use open questions to discover possibilities you may not have anticipated.

## Universal discovery prompts by cycle stage

| Stage | Prompts |
|---|---|
| **Observation and problem** | What is directly observed? What is inferred rather than observed? Who, where, and when are involved? What is the scale of the problem? What evidence would establish a useful baseline? |
| **Hypothesis and assumptions** | What explanations could account for the observation? Which assumptions connect the evidence to the explanation? What prediction follows from each explanation? What information is missing? |
| **Thesis and proposition** | What is the precise claim? What terms need defining? What is the claim’s scope? What evidence supports each part? What practical or logical consequences follow? |
| **Antithesis and counter-test** | What is the strongest alternative explanation? What evidence would count against the claim? Could selection bias, measurement error, or an unexamined factor explain the result? Who might reasonably disagree, and why? |
| **Synthesis and next inquiry** | Which parts of the claim remain supported? What must be revised or discarded? What uncertainty remains? What new observation or test would be most informative? |

## Architecture discovery questions

Use these when investigating a building, place, or built environment. Adapt them to the project’s scale and purpose.

| Area | Questions |
|---|---|
| **Purpose and success** | What should the building enable? Who defines success? What evidence would show that the building is working well? |
| **People and use** | Who uses, operates, maintains, or is affected by the building? What activities occur, and how do they vary by time, season, or user group? |
| **Site and context** | What conditions shape the site—climate, terrain, sunlight, noise, access, neighboring uses, or local history? Where are the main opportunities and constraints? |
| **Movement and access** | How do people arrive, enter, navigate, and leave? Where could routes conflict or become inaccessible? How do different mobility and sensory needs affect the plan? |
| **Space and relationships** | Which activities need to be near one another or kept apart? How much space is required, and what evidence supports that estimate? |
| **Structure and materials** | What structural approach and materials fit the use, site, budget, and intended lifespan? What are their maintenance demands, environmental impacts, and failure risks? |
| **Comfort and health** | How will light, air, temperature, acoustics, privacy, and views affect occupants? Which conditions should be controlled, and which can respond to local climate? |
| **Safety and resilience** | What hazards should the building withstand? How will people evacuate? How could essential functions continue during power, water, or communication disruptions? |
| **Rules and constraints** | Which building codes, permits, accessibility requirements, heritage conditions, or site restrictions apply? Which constraints are fixed, and which need further investigation? |
| **Cost and delivery** | What are the construction, operation, and maintenance costs? Which decisions drive cost most? What trade-offs would preserve the project’s essential goals? |
| **Environmental impact** | Where will the largest energy, water, material, and waste impacts occur over the building’s life? What can be reused, reduced, repaired, or adapted? |
| **Change over time** | How might users, technology, climate, or the surrounding area change? Which parts should be easy to adapt, repair, or replace? |
| **Evaluation** | What should be measured after occupation? Who will collect the evidence, when will performance be reviewed, and how will findings inform future changes? |

## Design discovery questions

Use these to investigate a designed object, service, system, experience, or process.

| Area | Questions |
|---|---|
| **Problem definition** | What problem is the design intended to solve? Is that the underlying problem or a visible symptom? How is the problem currently evidenced? |
| **Users and context** | Who experiences the problem, in what situation, and while trying to do what? Who is overlooked or affected indirectly? |
| **Research and assumptions** | What have users said or done that supports the current understanding? Which assumptions remain untested? What evidence could disprove them? |
| **Requirements and priorities** | What must the design do, and what is optional? When requirements conflict, which takes priority and why? |
| **Alternatives** | What different approaches could address the problem? What does each improve, worsen, cost, or make harder? |
| **Interaction and clarity** | What will users notice first? What will they expect to happen next? Where might they hesitate, make an error, or need help? |
| **Inclusion and accessibility** | Who might be excluded by the current approach? How does it work for people with different abilities, languages, experience, or access to technology? |
| **Feasibility and constraints** | What technical, financial, legal, or operational limits apply? Which constraint is most uncertain, and could it change the solution? |
| **Prototyping and testing** | What is the riskiest assumption? What is the smallest useful test? What result would lead you to change or reject the design? |
| **Trade-offs** | What does each design choice optimize, and what does it sacrifice? Are the trade-offs acceptable to the people affected? |
| **Failure and recovery** | What could go wrong in ordinary or unusual use? How will users notice, recover, or get assistance? |
| **Launch and maintenance** | Who will implement, support, and update the design? What ongoing work and resources will it require? |
| **Outcomes** | What observable result would indicate improvement? How will you determine whether the design contributed to that result? |

## Compact working template

Copy and fill this in for any topic:

| Cycle stage | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Observation** | What happened? Where and when? What is directly observed? |
| **Problem** | What needs explaining or changing? Who is affected? |
| **Hypothesis / assumptions** | What might explain it? What are we assuming? |
| **Thesis / proposition** | What is our precise claim, and where does it apply? |
| **Antithesis / counter-test** | What is the strongest challenge? What evidence would change our minds? |
| **Synthesis** | What does the evidence support now? What should be revised? |
| **Next observation or test** | What is the most useful next step, and what result would matter? |
