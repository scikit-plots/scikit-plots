- Corpus can measure whether scores presented as probabilities of relevance
  behave like probabilities: ``brier_score``, ``binary_log_loss``,
  ``reliability_table``, ``expected_calibration_error`` and
  ``calibration_report`` take scikit-learn's ``(y_true, y_prob)`` order, keep
  empty bins in the reliability table so two tables line up, refuse graded
  labels instead of thresholding them, and need no scikit-learn (their
  results equal ``brier_score_loss``, ``log_loss`` and
  ``calibration_curve``).
  ``LogOddsFusion`` combines retriever legs whose scores have fitted
  calibrators into probabilities of relevance: each leg's evidence
  ``logit(p) - logit(prevalence)``, weighted, is added to ``logit(base_rate)``
  (Bayes' rule for legs independent given relevance), a leg that did not list a
  document adds nothing, and an unfitted calibrator is refused. Each fused item
  carries its per-leg contributions, and the result reports the marginal
  correlation of every pair of legs' evidence. Rank fusion (``FusionPolicy``)
  stays the default.
  Hybrid search fuses this way when given
  ``RetrievalConfig(match_mode="hybrid", probability_fusion=ProbabilityFusion(policy, calibrators))``:
  each leg's native score (BM25, cosine) is calibrated by that leg's
  calibrator, hits are scored by the fused probability
  (``native_metric="logodds_probability"``), leg weights come from the policy
  (``hybrid_alpha`` must stay at its default), and an index bound to a
  ``SecurityFilter`` scope is refused because its BM25 statistics differ from
  the ones the calibrator was fitted on.
  ``CutPolicy`` keeps a fixed count, every hit at or above a probability
  threshold (refused on scores outside ``[0, 1]``) or the hits above the
  largest score gap, clamped by ``min_k``/``max_k``, and reports its evidence
  in a ``CutResult``. ``CachedSearch`` is an exact, bounded, thread-safe cache
  in front of an index, keyed by the index's content generation, its security
  filter, the query, every config field, the caller's filter and the query
  embedding; only ``success`` and ``empty`` responses are kept, and hits come
  back as copies.
